01 / Framework

A world model that predicts, a policy that anticipates.

A recurrent world model, jointly trained with PPO, turns proprioception and a single onboard depth stream into a predictive recurrent feature—no offboard perception, no terrain map—so a Unitree G1 clears gaps, stepping stones, stairs, and balance beams.

A

Predict

An RSSM encodes onboard depth and proprioception into a recurrent latent state and learns action-conditioned dynamics that predict future proprioception, egocentric depth, and task reward.

B

Condition

A projection of the recurrent memory, fWMt = gψ(ht), feeds the PPO actor–critic. Depth reaches the policy only through this predictive feature.

C

Train jointly

One stage: LPPO + LWM from the start, without world-model pretraining. At deployment the actor acts directly—no model rollout, no action search.

02 / Abstract

Abstract

  • Humanoid Locomotion
  • World Models
  • Foothold-Constrained Terrain
  • Reinforcement Learning

Foothold-constrained terrain limits feasible footholds to sparse, discontinuous, or narrow support regions, as in gaps, stepping stones, stairs, and balance beams. In such settings, a single misplaced step can cause a fall. Reliable traversal therefore requires precise foot placement and anticipation of upcoming terrain.

To address this challenge, we present World-Model-Augmented Visual Locomotion (WM-LOCO), which augments a visual locomotion policy with predictive context from a jointly trained world model. Given onboard depth observations and proprioception, a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) learns action-conditioned latent dynamics that predict future proprioceptive states, egocentric observations, and task rewards. The resulting recurrent feature is then fed into the proximal policy optimization (PPO) actor–critic, providing predictive context for anticipatory foot placement on foothold-constrained terrain.

WM-LOCO achieves average success rates of 96.0% on gaps, 96.9% on stepping stones, 94.0% on stairs, and 92.1% on balance beams, outperforming the evaluated baselines while improving gait efficiency and smoothness. Furthermore, WM-LOCO achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1, with 92.5% success across these four terrain classes.

Baselines

Comparison with GLAD and CReF

WM-LOCO is the only method whose mean success exceeds 90% on every terrain class. Its four-terrain mean is 94.8%, compared with 71.5% for GLAD and 77.6% for CReF. GLAD degrades on stepping stones and beams, whereas CReF degrades most strongly on gaps.

Combined course

All four terrain classes in one episode: 95.4% success

Ablation

The world model is what makes gaps possible

The complete model achieves 98.6–99.6% success across the tested gap widths. Removing the world model reduces success to 0% for every width from 0.4–0.5 m onward; removing the MoE backbone also produces near-total failure beyond the smallest gap range, while removing the history encoder mainly impairs wider-gap traversal.

World-model validation

Multi-step prediction without future observations

Given recorded actions, the world model predicts 15 updates (1.5 s) ahead without future observations. Its predicted depth stays closer to the Isaac Lab measurements than controls that repeat the initial observation or supply alternative actions, across the whole horizon. At 1.5 s, joint-position and joint-velocity errors remain close to the current-observation reconstruction reference; at 1.0 s, reward prediction reaches 0.072 MAE with 0.887 correlation.

Reconstruction reference

The latent state keeps the current observation

As a decoding reference, posterior reconstruction encodes and decodes the current observation at each update without a multi-step rollout. Errors remain low overall, with transient increases in depth and joint velocity, indicating that the latent state preserves information about the current observation.

Gait quality on stairs

Similar success, more efficient and smoother motion

On stairs both methods achieve high success, but WM-LOCO produces more efficient and smoother motion. Stride length increases by 25.6%, and pelvis acceleration decreases by 28.9%; these percentages are mean relative changes across the three tiers. Steps per meter and normalized mechanical energy also decrease at every tier.

05 / Method

Method details

A recurrent world model jointly trained with PPO, supplying a predictive recurrent feature to the actor–critic.

01

Observations

At each control step the robot observes a short proprioceptive history pt (joint positions, joint velocities, base angular velocity, projected gravity, and previous actions), a depth image dt from a single head-mounted camera, and a commanded base-frame velocity ct. A history encoder embeds the proprioceptive inputs of the last H = 5 control steps. On foothold-constrained terrain a foot landing outside the available support can directly cause a fall, so the policy must anticipate upcoming support conditions.

02

World Model

A recurrent state-space model (RSSM) with deterministic memory ht and a discrete stochastic latent zt: a GRU transition advances the memory with the actions executed since the previous update, a prior predicts zt from the memory alone, a posterior additionally uses the current observation, and prediction heads reconstruct proprioception and depth and predict the task reward. A KL term matches the prior to the posterior; since the prior has no access to the current observation, this trains the dynamics to predict observation-informed states from preceding states and actions.

03

Joint Training

The joint objective is LPPO + LWM. Training starts jointly without world-model pretraining, and world-model gradients also update the observation encoders. At each control step a projection network maps the most recently updated memory to the policy feature fWMt = gψ(ht); proprioception and the velocity command enter the actor–critic directly, while depth enters only through fWMt. The world model updates at 10 Hz and the policy at 50 Hz. At deployment the actor outputs actions directly, without model rollout or action search.

04

Training Rewards

A standard locomotion reward combines velocity and heading tracking with posture, gait, smoothness, energy, and joint-safety terms. Contact-region rewards, computed from foot-mesh sample points following Hiking in the Wild, discourage invalid foot placements: stairs use a boundary penalty, a riser-penetration penalty, and a sequential tread-contact reward; stepping stones use a top-contact reward whose window opens once per episode on sufficient contact and closes after a short duration or when contact is lost, after which the stone leaves the eligible set. The PPO baseline uses the same rewards.
