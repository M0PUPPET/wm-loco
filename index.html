Sim-to-Real

The trained policy is exported to ONNX and runs fully onboard the robot, from proprioception and a single head-mounted depth stream — no offboard perception, no precomputed terrain map, no extra state estimator.

Platform

Unitree G1 · single head-mounted Intel RealSense D435 depth stream · ONNX policy running onboard a Jetson AGX Orin for the results reported in the paper.

Terrains

Stepping stones with 0.25 m top surfaces and 0.45 m longitudinal gaps, stairs with 0.15 m risers and 0.25 m treads, a 0.8 m gap, and a 3 m long, 0.10 m wide balance beam.

Outcome

92.5% average success over 10 trials per class (37/40) — stepping stones 10/10, stairs 9/10, gap 9/10, balance beam 9/10.

The simulation-trained policy runs on hardware without additional tuning. Larger gaps were not tested, for safety.

Abstract

One predictive recurrent feature, one depth stream, four foothold-constrained classes.

Foothold-constrained terrain limits feasible footholds to sparse, discontinuous, or narrow support regions, as in gaps, stepping stones, stairs, and balance beams. In such settings, a single misplaced step can cause a fall. Reliable traversal therefore requires precise foot placement and anticipation of upcoming terrain.

We present World-Model-Augmented Visual Locomotion (WM-LOCO), which augments a visual locomotion policy with predictive context from a jointly trained world model. Given onboard depth observations and proprioception, a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) learns action-conditioned latent dynamics that predict future proprioceptive states, egocentric observations, and task rewards. The resulting recurrent feature is then fed into the PPO actor–critic, providing predictive context for anticipatory foot placement on foothold-constrained terrain.

WM-LOCO achieves average success rates of 96.0% on gaps, 96.9% on stepping stones, 94.0% on stairs, and 92.1% on balance beams, outperforming the evaluated baselines while improving gait efficiency and smoothness. Furthermore, WM-LOCO achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1, with 92.5% success across these four terrain classes.

Method

A recurrent world model jointly trained with PPO, supplying a predictive recurrent feature to the actor–critic.

01

Problem

Locomotion as a POMDP. At each control step the policy observes a 5-frame proprioception history (joint positions, joint velocities, base angular velocity, projected gravity, previous actions), a depth image from a single head-mounted camera, and a commanded base-frame velocity. It tracks the command while traversing the terrain and avoiding non-foot contacts; where feasible contacts are sparse, one infeasible step can leave little room to recover before a fall.

02

World Model

A recurrent state-space model (RSSM) with deterministic memory ht and a discrete stochastic latent zt: a transition updates the memory with the actions since the previous update, a posterior infers zt from the memory and the current observation, a prior predicts zt from the memory alone, and decoders reconstruct proprioception and depth while a reward head predicts the task reward. The KL term regularizes the prior toward the posterior, so ht retains information useful for predicting the latent before the current observation arrives.

03

Joint Training

The PPO clipped surrogate and LWM are optimized in a single joint update—no separate pretraining stage, no offline replay buffer—with world-model gradients propagating into the shared observation encoders. At each control step a projection network maps the latest memory to the policy feature fWMt = gψ(ht); proprioception and the velocity command enter the actor–critic directly, while depth enters only through fWMt. The world model updates at 10 Hz and the policy at 50 Hz. Inference requires no imagined rollouts, and no foothold labels are used.

04

Reward Shaping

A standard velocity-tracking objective is too sparse here, so terrain-specific shaping terms are added—identically for WM-LOCO and PPO within each terrain-training setting—computed from a foot volume-point set following the sensor design of Hiking in the Wild. Stairs use a boundary penalty, a riser-penetration penalty, and a sequential tread-contact reward; stepping stones use a separate parallel formulation in which every valid stone top is active at reset and consumed independently after qualifying contact.

Figure 2 · WM-LOCO jointly trains an RSSM and a PPO actor–critic; the recurrent feature supplies predictive context to the policy. Proprioception and the velocity command enter the actor–critic directly, while depth enters only through the world-model feature fWMt; a mixture-of-experts backbone fuses the inputs and feeds the actor and critic heads. The PPO baseline omits only the world-model pathway and its auxiliary loss.

Comparison with GLAD and CReF

WM-LOCO is the only method whose mean success exceeds 90% on every terrain class. Its four-terrain mean is 94.8%, compared with 71.5% for GLAD and 77.6% for CReF. GLAD degrades on stepping stones and beams, whereas CReF degrades most strongly on gaps.

Combined course

On a combined course that chains all four terrain classes in one episode, WM-LOCO achieves 95.4% success in simulation.

Figure 1(d) · Stair ascent, stepping stones, a gap, a balance beam, and stair descent in one simulated episode.

Ablation on gap terrain

The complete model achieves 98.6–99.6% success across the tested gap widths. Removing the world model reduces success to 0% for every width from 0.4–0.5 m onward; removing the MoE backbone also produces near-total failure beyond the smallest gap range, while removing the history encoder mainly impairs wider-gap traversal.

Figure 5 · Gap-traversal success of the full model and of variants without the world model, history encoder, or MoE.

World-model validation

Given recorded actions, the world model predicts 15 updates (1.5 s) ahead without future observations. Its predicted depth stays closer to the Isaac Lab measurements than controls that repeat the initial observation or supply alternative actions, across the whole horizon. At 1.5 s, joint-position and joint-velocity errors remain close to the current-observation reconstruction reference; at 1.0 s, reward prediction reaches 0.072 MAE with 0.887 correlation.

Figure 3 · Multi-step world-model predictions on stepping stones: (a) depth sequences; (b, c) depth and proprioceptive errors. Rollouts receive no future observations.

Gait quality on stairs

On stairs both methods achieve high success, but WM-LOCO produces more efficient and smoother motion: stride length improves while steps per metre, pelvis acceleration, and normalized mechanical energy all decrease.
